# Concrete Delivery Operations Intelligence System

## 1. Project Overview

Build a professional, production-style web application called:

**Concrete Delivery Operations Intelligence System**

The system is designed for a ready-mix concrete plant to monitor, analyze, and improve the performance of concrete deliveries.

The initial implementation is based on a Peliyagoda concrete plant operation, but the architecture must be generic enough to support multiple plants, projects, customers, trucks, and operational users in the future.

The core business problem is:

> Concrete deliveries are planned around estimated departure and delivery times, but actual deliveries may be delayed because of traffic, plant loading, truck availability, site waiting, route issues, weather, or other operational factors.

The system should convert raw delivery information into actionable operational intelligence.

### Core workflow

**Order → Scheduling → Plant Loading → Departure → Transit → Site Arrival → Delay Analysis → Operational Insight**

The application should answer:

* How many deliveries were completed?
* How many were on time?
* How many were delayed?
* How severe were the delays?
* What is the average delay?
* Which projects experience the most delays?
* Which trucks experience recurring delays?
* Which days/times have the highest delays?
* What are the most common delay causes?
* Are delays mainly happening at the plant, during transportation, or at the customer site?
* Which deliveries require immediate attention?
* Are operational performance levels improving over time?

---

# 2. Design Philosophy

Do not build this as a generic admin dashboard template.

It should look like a serious **industrial operations monitoring system** used by a concrete plant or logistics operation.

Visual direction:

* Professional
* Modern
* Minimal
* Industrial
* Data-focused
* High information density without looking cluttered
* Excellent spacing and hierarchy
* Responsive
* Desktop-first but fully usable on tablets
* Clean typography
* Subtle animations
* Professional charts
* Strong visual distinction between normal, warning, and critical conditions

Use a sophisticated navy / blue / cyan visual system inspired by engineering and industrial monitoring interfaces.

Avoid:

* Excessive gradients
* Excessive glassmorphism
* Cartoon-style illustrations
* Huge decorative elements
* AI-generated-looking UI
* Generic SaaS dashboard appearance
* Excessive rounded cards
* Excessive shadows
* Unnecessary animations

Use **Poppins** or another highly readable modern sans-serif font.

# 62. Important UX Principle

The system should follow this hierarchy:

### Level 1

**What is happening?**

KPIs and status.

### Level 2

**Where is the problem?**

Stage, project, truck, time period.

### Level 3

**Why is it happening?**

Delay reason analysis.

### Level 4

**Which specific deliveries are responsible?**

Drill-down.

### Level 5

**What should management investigate?**

Operational insights and exceptions.

This hierarchy is more important than simply adding more charts.

# 63. Prototype Requirements

The first implementation must be fully functional without requiring a backend.

Use:

* React
* TypeScript
* CSS/Tailwind
* Chart.js or Recharts
* Local Storage
* CSV import/export

Include a demo dataset.

Every dashboard number must be calculated from the underlying delivery records.

Do not hardcode KPI values.

For example, if the dataset contains 248 records, calculate:

```text
totalDeliveries = deliveries.length
```

Calculate on-time performance from actual records.

Calculate average delay from actual calculated delivery delays.

Calculate delay reason statistics dynamically.
